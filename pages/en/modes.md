# Modes and full-board challenges

Choose who to play with, then how to win. Matchmaking, board difficulty, task assignment, victory rules and seed source are separate settings; a ranked label on one setting does not define the whole match.

## Choosing a mode

| Mode | Best for | Objective | Public rating |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ranked queue | Competitive duels | Standard 5×5; first to 15 goals | Rated |
| Casual queue | Relaxed matches and races | Classic, race or Most% rules | Unrated |
| Custom rooms | Solo, friends and groups | Host-selected rules | Unrated |
| Full-board challenge | Completing the whole board | Finish every valid goal | Private rooms are unrated |
| DRAFTOUT | Private competitive duels | Exclusive goals; first to 13 | Unrated |

## Custom play: a ruleset, not a rated queue

Create a room to invite friends or practise alone. Available combinations depend on solo, duel or multiplayer play. Random assignment generates a board, drafting lets players choose, and custom assignment lets the host fill it. See [Custom rooms](#rooms) for permissions, settings and presets.

## Full-board challenge

Challenge is a victory condition: finish the board rather than the standard 15 goals. Complete 3×3, 5×5 and 7×7 boards contain 9, 25 and 49 goals. This does not automatically select hard tasks or create a rated match. Victory rules and task difficulty are independent.

Use it for solo practice, private duels or compatible multiplayer rules. BINGO is a separate condition requiring a valid line. If a host leaves custom cells empty, check that the resulting objective is achievable; empty cells do not become randomly filled tasks.

## Races and timed progress

Portal and treasure races use rounds and their own score targets, rather than 15 goals or a full board. Most% compares progress within a time limit. Read [Casual modes](#casual) and [Race rules](#races), then check the current in-game objective.

> This handbook follows the latest production release. Unavailable room combinations, test-server experiments and unreleased fixes are not production features merely because they are documented.
